03 / Actual training sessions

Start every running session with an 8–10 minute warm-up.
BEFORE EVERY RUN
2–3 min easy jog or brisk walk
Ankle circles and leg swings
Walking lunges, gentle high knees and easy running drills
MONDAY · TECHNIQUE

Sprint mechanics

Relaxed, controlled movements.

  • A-march: 2 × 15 m
  • A-skips: 2 × 15 m
  • High knees: 2 × 10 sec
  • Relaxed strides: 4 × 20 m
Walk back and recover
THURSDAY · ACCELERATION

Start practice

Learn to accelerate smoothly.

  • Standing start practice: 3 reps
  • Short accelerations: 4 × 10 m
  • Walk fully between reps
  • Finish with 3 min walking
No all-out racing
SATURDAY · EASY MOVEMENT

Running comfort

Build general movement confidence.

  • Easy jog: 5–8 min
  • Comfortable runs: 3 × 30 m
  • Gentle coordination drills: 5 min
  • Cool-down walk: 3–5 min
Finish comfortably
TUESDAY + FRIDAY · LIGHT STRENGTH

Bodyweight basics

  • Bodyweight squats: 2 × 8
  • Glute bridges: 2 × 10
  • Calf raises: 2 × 10
  • Reverse lunges: 1 × 8 each leg
  • Plank: 2 × 15 sec
  • Single-leg balance: 2 × 15 sec each
Rest between sets. Keep movements controlled and stop if anything hurts.

04 / Four-week progression

Small steps. No rushing the process.
WEEK 1

Build the habit

  • Learn the warm-up
  • Practise sprint mechanics
  • Use short, comfortable accelerations
  • Record your starting point
GOAL · Complete planned sessions comfortably.
WEEK 2

Improve coordination

  • Repeat the same three running sessions
  • Improve arm and leg coordination
  • Make running feel more relaxed
  • Continue light strength training
GOAL · Better technique and consistency.
WEEK 3

Develop speed control

  • Only if recovering well, progress short accelerations to 15–20 m
  • Maintain full walking recovery
  • Practise smooth running transitions
  • Do not add reps just to feel tired
GOAL · Better acceleration technique.
WEEK 4

Consolidate and evaluate

  • Reduce training volume slightly
  • Review your technique
  • Repeat a supervised short-distance timing test if appropriate
GOAL · Finish feeling stronger, not exhausted.

05 / Track progress wisely

Use the same conditions when comparing results.

Ask your PE teacher to help measure a short-distance time (for example, 30 m) on the same surface and under similar conditions.

You do not need to repeatedly test your maximum 150 m time. Save full race testing for supervised practice when your coach considers you ready.

The event may be 150 m or longer. Once the school confirms the distance, the later training blocks can be adjusted.

07 / Three rules to follow

Your health comes before any race result.
01Never compare your daily training volume with your competitor's.
02Never sacrifice sleep or recovery to squeeze in extra training.
03Never ignore pain, dizziness or unusual fatigue. Stop and tell a trusted adult or coach.

Before you begin

Use a flat, dry, clear field and suitable footwear. Ask your PE teacher or a qualified athletics coach to check your technique before harder sprinting. Drink water, eat regular balanced meals and aim for 8–10 hours of sleep. If you feel unwell or something hurts, skip the session and tell a trusted adult.
